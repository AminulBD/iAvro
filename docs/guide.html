Getting started

  1. Choose Avro Keyboard from the input menu in the menu bar, or switch to it with your input source shortcut (Control Space or 🌐 by default).
  2. Pick a layout under Preferences… › Keyboard Layout. Avro Phonetic is the default; the rest of this section is about it, and Probhat and Unijoy follow.
  3. Type. The rules below show what each key gives.

The candidate bar

On Windows, Avro shows what you type in a preview window. On the Mac, the word is shown underlined in your text as you type, with a bar of candidates under it:

  1. Your own replacement or a built-in auto-correct entry, if there is one
  2. What you typed, unchanged, to keep an English word
  3. Dictionary words that match, closest spelling first
  4. The plain transliteration, made by the rules on this page, and matching emoji

Space or Return commits the highlighted word. The arrow keys move through the candidates, and 1–9 pick one by its number. Delete removes the last letter you typed.

To see just the rules at work, turn off Include Dictionary Suggestions in Preferences. The bar then shows only the transliteration and what you typed.

Forcing a full vowel or a sign

Now and then Avro picks the wrong one. eki → একি, but একই sounds almost the same and needs a full ই after the ক. Type the backtick `, the key above Tab to the left of 1, between them: ek`i → একই.

Rule 4: Conjuncts

There is nothing extra to learn. When consonants are typed together and form a conjunct in Bangla, Avro joins them:

okkhor → অক্ষরonto → অন্তbiggo → বিজ্ঞbrahmoN → ব্রাহ্মণlokkhmI → লক্ষ্মীsombol → সম্বল

The full conjunct table below lists every conjunct and how to type it.

Keeping consonants apart

Sometimes two consonants that could join shouldn’t. ন and ত join in কান্তা and কিন্তু, but not in কিনতে. Type a backtick ` between the two letters to keep them apart:

Joined, as wantedJoined by mistakeKept apart with `
গ্লglOb → গ্লোবaglanO → আগ্লানোag`lanO → আগলানো
ক্টokTOpas → অক্টোপাসekTa → এক্টাek`Ta → একটা
ম্রmriyomaN → ম্রিয়মাণamra → আম্রাam`ra → আমরা

You rarely need this. With dictionary suggestions on, the candidate bar usually offers the right word anyway: amra offers আমরা first, and ekta gives একটা from the built-in auto-correct.

Avro Phonetic has no rule for ক্ম. Pick a word with it from the dictionary, like rukmini → রুক্মিণী, or add it as a personal replacement.

Auto-correct

Some words don’t follow the rules: English words written in Bangla, names, and spellings the rules get wrong. Auto-correct handles them, and its entry comes first in the candidate bar.

Built-in: about 10,000 entries ship with Avro, like sir → স্যার, software → সফটওয়্যার and brb → একটু পরে আসছি. They are listed under Preferences… › AutoCorrect › Built-in, and are offered with dictionary suggestions turned on.

Your own: add entries under Preferences… › AutoCorrect › Personal. Click +, type the shortcut, press Return, then type what it should become. They come first, even with dictionary suggestions turned off. Some uses:

  • Fix a word the rules get wrong. amra → আম্রা joins ম and র into ম্র. Add amra → আমরা and it always comes out right.
  • Keep an English spelling. Make zoom give জুম, the way software → সফটওয়্যার already does.
  • Type long text with a short word. Make enam give your full name, enamul hok cOUdhurI → এনামুল হক চৌধুরী, or brb give a whole sentence.

More on this in Your own replacements.

Probhat and Unijoy

With a fixed layout, every key types one Bangla letter or sign, so you type what you see. Choose one under Preferences… › Keyboard Layout.

  • Keys are read as they are on a US keyboard, so keep ABC or U.S. as your other input source.
  • There is no candidate bar: what you type goes straight into the text.
  • The Option (AltGr) layer of the Windows layouts isn’t included yet.
